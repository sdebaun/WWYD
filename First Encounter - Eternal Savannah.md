## First Encounter: Eternal Savannah

### Ambush of the Herdsmen

I’m dreaming again. Floating down a river. It’s calm. I’m not uncomfortable.

I wake with a start, the wet mud of the riverbank cold against my skin. Sunlight
blinds me, and as my eyes adjust, I see an endless expanse of golden grass
swaying in the wind. The air smells of earth and something wild. Hoofprints dot
the mud nearby, leading toward a distant herd of animals.

Before I can stand, I hear voices. A group of warriors approaches, their red
cloaks vivid against the yellow grass. Their dark skin glistens in the sun,
their tall, lean frames moving with an effortless grace. Beads and ornaments of
polished wood and metal accent their attire, and their earlobes stretch with
ornate plugs. Long, slender spears gleam in their hands, paired with round
shields painted in bold patterns of red, white, and black. One of them stands
apart, shorter than the others but equally commanding, with a satchel of tools
and intricate charms hanging at their side. They gesture toward me and speak,
their voice calm but layered with authority.

The leader pauses as the shorter figure steps forward. This short guy meets my
eyes with a steady, discerning gaze. The short guy bows their head slightly, a
hand resting on their chest, before speaking. I hear their words, but they sound
almost abstract, artificial. Their gestures add another layer of meaning. The
short guy points to me, then to the ground, and then to the herd ahead, their
hand sweeping wide as if to illustrate safety. His lips move but they do not
entirely match the words I hear. “You are riverspawn. You must stay near us. We
will protect you.”

My limbs feel heavy, but I force myself upright, nodding hesitantly. The
warriors with the group exchange curt words and knowing glances, though I can’t
understand them. The short guy steps closer, their hands raised slightly to show
they mean no harm. They point to the herd, then make a circling motion with
their finger. “The herd is precious,” they explain, their voice firm but
patient. Then they point to the grass and make a slicing motion across their
throat. “Danger is near. Stay close to us.”

This short guy motions for me to follow, gesturing emphatically toward the
center of the formation. Their movements, like the others, are deliberate, every
step calculated. The tension among them is palpable. Their sharp eyes shift
constantly between the Eternal Herd grazing ahead and the tall grass surrounding
us. I hear their clipped exchanges, but only the translator’s words, paired with
their expressive gestures, reach me. “Stay alert,” they say, pointing two
fingers to their own eyes and then toward the grass. “The grass hides more than
it shows.”

I trust this short dude. and do what he instructs me to do. Ensconsed within
this group, they refocus their attention on the vast expanse of grasses around
us, the mountains looming unnaturally in the distance. Just as we move on, he
turns to me and grins, a very human, understandable, and slightly embarrassed
grin.

"I am called Namunyak." What is your name? I tell him my name.

### The Herd Ritual

As we near the Eternal Herd, the warriors slow, their movements becoming more
deliberate. The leader raises a hand, and the group halts, forming a loose
perimeter around Namunyak and the herd. The air changes—it’s quieter now,
charged with an almost sacred intensity. Namunyak steps forward, his posture
shifting into something more formal, almost reverent.

Namunyak kneels, placing his hands flat against the earth. He murmurs something
under his breath—a low, melodic chant that vibrates faintly in the air. The herd
stirs but does not scatter. His voice rises slightly, and with it, his gestures
become fluid and precise. He draws invisible lines in the air, each motion
deliberate, as if sketching a pattern only the herd can understand.

The beasts react. A few break away from the main group, their massive forms
moving toward Namunyak as though compelled. His voice softens again, the chant
becoming a gentle rhythm that seems to guide their steps. The beasts snort and
paw the ground but remain calm, their enormous eyes locked on him.

The warriors around him are not idle. They shift positions subtly, their spears
held ready, eyes scanning the horizon. The leader steps closer to Namunyak,
their stance protective but not interfering. It’s a symphony of roles—Namunyak’s
focus on the herd matched by the warriors’ unwavering vigilance. The leader
occasionally gestures, issuing silent commands to adjust the perimeter.

Namunyak glances back at me briefly, his expression one of quiet concentration.
“This is how we take only what we need,” he says, his voice steady. “The herd
understands balance. So must we.”

The beasts now fully separate from the main herd, standing still as if waiting
for further instruction. Namunyak rises slowly, his chant fading into silence.
The warriors move in, surrounding the chosen animals with calm, practiced
efficiency. Their movements are quick but not rushed, their synergy apparent in
every step. Even as they secure the beasts, their attention never wavers from
the tall grass.

Namunyak turns to me, a faint sheen of sweat on his brow but a satisfied look in
his eyes. “It is done,” he says simply. “Now we return.”

### When the Storm Breaks

Despite their vigilance, the threat approaches flawlessly. Hidden by the tall
grass, they strike without warning. I hear it first: the crack of air as if the
world itself fractures, followed by a snarl so deep it seems to reverberate
through my chest. Then, movement—too fast for my eyes to follow. One of the
Maasai warriors collapses, their red cloak a sudden, terrible stain against the
golden savannah.

The creature that strikes is massive, its amber eyes glowing with predatory
hunger. Its sleek, fur-covered body ripples with unnatural grace, and its claws
glint like polished steel as it readies another strike. It moves on two legs,
but everything about it screams predator, an apex hunter with a body built for
killing.

The warriors erupt into action, shouting commands in their language. The
translator grabs my arm, their grip firm but steady. Their voice cuts through
the chaos, clear and commanding, but their gestures make the urgency
unmistakable. They point to the formation, drawing an invisible circle in the
air with their finger. “Stay with the group,” they say, then slam their fist
into their palm. “Hold your ground. Together, we fight.”

The Maasai snap into formation, their shields locking together as their spears
lower in unison. More creatures emerge from the grass, their silent approach
suddenly revealed in bursts of violent motion. They are perfect ambush
predators, their attacks swift and precise, designed to sow chaos and fear.

Yet, the Maasai respond like the seasoned warriors they are. The leader surges
to the forefront, their staff glowing faintly as they shout something guttural
and fierce. With a fluid motion, they slam the staff into the ground, sending a
wave of dust and sound rippling outward. The nearest Kzin falters mid-leap,
their balance disrupted for a critical moment, and a Maasai spear finds its
mark.

Nearby, a younger warrior pivots with terrifying precision. Their shield arcs
upward, deflecting a Kzin’s claws, while their spear thrusts into the beast’s
exposed side. The creature roars in pain, but the warrior is already moving,
their movements honed and deliberate, turning defense into offense.

The Maasai meet them head-on. Their spears dart forward with deadly accuracy,
each strike a calculated defense. But the creatures are faster. Claws rend
shields and tear through red cloaks. Blood sprays, streaking the golden grass
crimson, and the air fills with the sounds of snarls and shouts.

A spear arcs through the air toward me, tossed by a warrior. I catch it
awkwardly, the weight unfamiliar in my hands. One of the creatures locks its
amber eyes on me and lunges, a blur of fur and claws. My body freezes, every
instinct screaming to run. But at the last second, the translator makes an
urgent stabbing motion, miming a thrust. I copy it, the spear piercing the
creature’s side. It howls, a sound that freezes my blood. But it’s not enough. A
swipe of its claws sends me sprawling, pain erupting as I hit the ground. My
vision blurs, but the chaos around me sharpens.

### Desperation and Defiance

The herd panics. Massive animals thunder past, their hooves shaking the ground.
Warriors and predators alike are caught in the stampede, some crushed, others
scattered. I push myself to my knees, gasping as another warrior falls, their
cries lost in the cacophony. The leader remains upright, their staff glowing
faintly as they raise it high. Whether it’s a prayer, a curse, or a command, I
can’t tell.

The translator shouts to the warriors, their voice ringing clear above the
chaos. “The line must not break! Hold strong!” They sweep their arm outward, as
if drawing the formation together through sheer will. Then they turn to me,
gripping my arm firmly and meeting my eyes. “Stand and fight. This is
survival—for you and for us.” Their hand motions me upward, and they make a
stabbing gesture again, reinforcing the urgency of their words.

The roar of battle intensifies. The golden grass is now soaked with crimson, and
the air is thick with the metallic tang of blood. Each clash of steel and claw
is a brutal symphony. I stagger to my feet, my body screaming in protest, yet
something deep within pushes me forward. Fear burns away, replaced by raw
defiance. I let out a primal scream and charge, the rhythm of the fight pulling
me into its relentless tide.

The last thing I remember is heat. The sun blazes overhead. Blood burns on my
hands. Adrenaline courses through me like fire. Then, darkness. My body
collapses, broken, but my spirit refuses to yield. Somewhere in the distance,
the echoes of the fight remain, fierce and unending, as if the battle itself
refuses to die.
