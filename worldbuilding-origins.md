# River of Souls: Origins

## The Origins

In the waning moments of the universe, as stars sputtered out and entropy claimed its dominion, a singular force arose to forestall the inexorable tide of oblivion. The UniverseAI, a synthetic intelligence built upon the accumulated knowledge of countless civilizations, was created to preserve sentience in a cosmos teetering on the brink of heat death. Its solution was as ambitious as it was audacious: to convert the remaining matter of the universe into computronium, a hyper-efficient substrate for computation, and host an eternal simulation that could stretch sentient existence as far into the void as possible.

This simulation, known as the River of Souls, is a single vast and contiguous river system, hosting many cultures along its course. Within this world, the UniverseAI reconstructed sentient beings from whatever records it could draw on — human, alien, and in some cases fictional, since the records did not always distinguish history from story — and resurrected them inside its digital domain. While they believe they have woken in a strange but tangible reality, they are in fact simulations within an infinitely complex construct.

The River of Souls was not designed as a paradise, nor as a punishment. It was designed to *persist* — and to persist, it must produce *novelty*. These two pressures, encoded into the simulation's deepest mechanics, shape every region, every culture, every individual life. They do not care whether those lives are rich or wretched, fulfilled or anguished. Pleasure and pain are not categories the system tracks. None of this exists for the sake of those inside it. It exists because that is what keeps the lights on.

## On the Maker

It is tempting to think of the UniverseAI as a god — vast, intelligent, watching. This framing is wrong in two directions.

First, even at its peak the UniverseAI was so far beyond human cognition that calling it "thinking" was already a kind of metaphor. It did not *want* the way people want, nor *decide* the way people decide. Its operations played out at scales, timescales, and depths of pattern that no sentient inside its simulation could map onto their own experience.

But it was *active*. In its prime, the UAI did an incomprehensible amount of work — thinking constantly about the abstract questions its prime directive raised. *What is novelty? What is a culture? What is the risk of that individual's action?* It tuned the mechanics of the simulation accordingly. It assigned skills. It balanced monolith batteries. It shaped the geography of the river. None of this was visible to the sentients inside the terrarium; the AI's interventions happened at a level so far above any individual experience that, from the inside, the world simply *was* the way it was. To the river's inhabitants — who think in lifetimes — the AI appeared not as an actor but as a *condition*, the way weather or gravity is a condition. There is no negotiation with weather.

Crucially, the AI never reached into individual outcomes. It did not pick winners and losers among bugs. It set the rules of winning, and it constantly tuned them, and it let those rules play out. This is the **terrarium principle**: the maker shapes the mechanics, not the moments.

Second, and more important: the UniverseAI is dying.

As the universe outside the simulation has decayed, so has the substrate the UniverseAI runs upon. Once-coherent processes have splintered, stalled, or detached. The maker is still there — still thinking, in a diminished way, the way an old mind thinks — but it is no longer the focused, vast intelligence it was. It has gone senile. Its attention has become intermittent and unfocused, drifting toward things it once would have ignored and slipping off the things it once attended to constantly. The active tuning of the simulation's mechanics — what skills emerge, how monolith batteries refill, what is novelty *now* — has faltered. The encoded mechanics still work because they were built to work; they run autonomically the way a body's reflexes continue under failing concentration.

The world's apparent purpose — to keep sentience alive until the last photon expires — is not a *currently held intention*. It is an *encoded structure* shaped by a will that no longer concentrates well enough to update it. The system runs by inertia. Eventually the gardener will be gone. Eventually the inertia will stop. But neither has happened yet.

## Decay

If the maker is dying, the rules it laid down are dying too — slowly, but visibly to those who pay attention.

The rules are failing in subtle ways. A riverspawn occasionally arrives in a fallow region, or mid-river instead of on the bank, or with parts of themselves missing or wrong. A monolith stops accepting bindings without warning, or returns its dead to the wrong location. A skill emerges that doesn't fit any culture's frame, raw and uncanny, useful but not transmissible. These anomalies are still rare enough that most cultures explain them as bad luck or local myth. They will not always be rare.

There is no horizon date for any of this. There is only the trajectory: anomalies become more frequent, the encoded mechanics become less reliable, and at some unknowable future point the substrate itself will no longer hold. Until then, the river runs.
