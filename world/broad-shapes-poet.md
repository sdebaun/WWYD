This file is the Poet's running summary of the world as Stephen has told it in conversation. It is rewritten whole as the talk goes on. Text in quotes is Stephen's, word for word. Everything else is the Poet's summary. All quotes are from 2026-11-24.

## What is canon

"i have changed my mind or have had major reservations about much of the worldbuilding docs.  but i think everything in the actual chapters, still adheres to my vision."

## The broad shapes

His five points, as he gave them:

- "the saga takes place in time near the heat death of the universe"
- "all remaining matter and energy in the universe has been converted to computronium, and harnessed by an entity i called the WorldAI, or UniverseAI, but might start calling the superintelligence, or some other name that sounds better"
- "that superintelligence had its humble origins in the AI technology of the 21st century"
- "it is using the computronium to run a simulation of a vast world, in which it is trying to bring to life every being it thinks existed"
- "key word: *thinks* existed.  in the fragmented, incomplete records of early time, it cannot tell the difference between fact and fiction.  its records contain more about the roadrunner and wile coyote than it does a random masaai tribesman"

The name of the entity is not settled.

## The machine itself

Its motive: "its prime directive, the core motive, the foundational essenece that it ended up with before the long tail, is that all these beings must be preserved"

Its condition: "its been going a while, and its starting to decay, so its like, a slightly senior universeAI.  forgetting where its keys are and such"

The shape of the idea, in his words: "a beautiful inversion of The Matrix trope"

## How the machine reads its records

"yes the machine builds each being as its record shows it / but in some way it also realizes that its records are interpretations.  it just doesnt understand that they might be fictional"

His example: "a picture of a cave painting, its going to read as aurochs existed, and that prehistoric humans hunted them, and when coupled with all the scholarly lit it has in its records, its going to come up with simulations of both that are close enough to real to be indistinguishable from real."

And: "sufficiently advanced technology, and all that"

In short: the machine looks through a record to the thing it thinks the record is about. It does not copy the record's surface.

## Why it brings back fictional beings

Stephen named the problem: "the SI can brilliantly infer a sim-auroch from all these ancient records; but those same ancient records dont help it realize that the damn thing is fictional".

The answer he had been thinking of, which the Poet put this way at 18:26 and he confirmed at 18:32 ("yeah the third is where i had been thinking"): the machine will not bet a life on the difference. Its task is to preserve everyone. If it calls a real being fictional, that being stays dead forever. If it calls a fictional being real, the cost is one extra being. So where it is not certain, it brings them back.

Two reasons it is never certain, offered by the Poet and not yet ruled on by Stephen:

- The labels burned. "Fiction" is a shelf mark in a catalogue, not a property of a text, and the records came down in fragments. Content cannot sort them: sincere ancient records hold omens and gods, and invented ones hold plausible people.
- It inherited the blind spot from its 21st-century origins, which learned the world from one pile of text where real and invented people are learned the same way.

## The veils

At 19:30 Stephen sent the Poet to read one legacy doc, `legacy/worldbuilding-the-veils.md`, and only for the veils, to correct the Poet's reading of the Narn. The rest of `legacy/` stays unread. Which details of that doc still stand is his to say. Until he does, this file holds only the frame, in the Poet's summary:

- First Veil: people see how the river works and explain it in the terms of the culture they came from. Most never leave this. Power does not depend on leaving it.
- Second Veil: a person stops taking their own culture's frame for the truth, sees the different frames as masks on one face, and can work on the rules themselves. They know how, not why.
- Third Veil: a person knows the foundation. The world is simulated, everyone is a reconstruction from records, some were rebuilt from stories, and none can know which they are. People answer that knowledge in very different ways.

How this bears on the Narn: Stephen said of the Poet's reading (that the Narn sides with the ewoks because his people were once the occupied), "not quite on the narn / like its related, but not just like that". The Poet now reads the Narn's "next-level of awareness" as a veil pierced, and his "we" as others who have pierced it. Which veil, and what that "we" wants, Stephen has not said.

## Everyone is rebuilt, the narrator too

Stephen, 19:40, correcting the Poet, who had said the narrator knows which beings are fictional: "*everyone* is a rebuilt one right?  and *everyone* starts with the belief that they are real"

"the glacial reveal for the ontology of the narrator", in his words and his order:

- "obviously, im real, like why would you even bring this up"
- "shit, is that real"
- "shit, thats a fucking cartoon monster"
- "shit, thats an actual human being "just like me""

The narrator can tell which of the others came from stories. He cannot tell it of himself.

## "Aberration"

Settled by Stephen, 18:43: "that is just the word that the christofacists use / the mexica probably have another private word they use for it, out of earshot of the christofacists / but from the perspective of the SI, the coyote & roadrunner are just as real to it, as the masaai, and the mexica, and the ewoks, and the narn, etc"

So the word is a local label, the empire's. It is not a category of the world. To the machine there is one kind of being.

How the empire uses it, 18:51: "the general category is aberration.  that they will just throw at the most incomprehensible avatars that show up in their stretch of the river.  they will have specific labels for ones they are familiar with." For the coyote and roadrunner: "the clergy call this one the Fast and Fool", and the troopers call it "double-f".

What the locals see: "these absolutely terrifying monsters that run amok". The patrol skirts them "while the local heavy hitters deal with it."

The Mexica's private word is not chosen.
