- "I have no idea what the hell he just said, but I know *exactly* how he feels about it." This replaced "it terrifies me". The fear is now the squaddie's, read by the narrator, and the narrator stays dry enough for the joke that follows. The conflict I raised with his panic rule is gone.

## What the scene is for

"this should be part of the introduction of the world to the reader.  another 'wtf' moment, theres the literal coyote and roadrunner from the cartoons, what is this world.  because the secret narn and the subtle kzin may not have been enough to clue the reader in"

So this is the tell nobody can miss. The kzin and the Narn reward a reader who already knows them. The coyote is for every reader.

## What the empire calls them

Three levels, in his words: "the general category is aberration.  that they will just throw at the most incomprehensible avatars that show up in their stretch of the river.  they will have specific labels for ones they are familiar with."

- The category: "aberration". It is "just the word that the christofacists use".
- The clergy's name for this pair: "the clergy call this one the Fast and Fool."
- The troopers' name: "double-f".
- The Mexica: "probably have another private word they use for it, out of earshot of the christofacists". The word is Huehuecóyotl, the old coyote, the trickster god of the Nahua. On the page the narrator hears it as "way way COY tell". "Huehue" is old and "coyotl" is coyote, the word English borrowed through Spanish. The real sound is roughly weh-weh-KOY-otl, with the last "tl" as one sound and no vowel after it.

The empire is not on the coyote's side: "both of those fuckers just destroy everything around them".

To the machine none of these words apply: "from the perspective of the SI, the coyote & roadrunner are just as real to it, as the masaai, and the mexica, and the ewoks, and the narn, etc"

Notes of mine on the names:

- The creed backs "Fool" twice. Proverbs 26:11: "As a dog returneth to his vomit, so a fool returneth to his folly." Proverbs 17:12: "Let a bear robbed of her whelps meet a man, rather than a fool in his folly."
- "A fast" in this creed is also going without food, so the bird carries the name of the thing the coyote is doing.
- A man from the early twenty-first century will hear a film title in "the Fast and the".
- Troopers would hear their own meaning in the two letters of "double-f", and it would not be the clergy's.
- The narrator can hear the names before he sees the thing. He can also hear a low word in a language he does not have, and notice it stop.

## What they look like

Ruled, 18:58: "yes and it looks realistic".

His own second option was "it look "real", just an anthropormorphic but realistic looking coyote, and an elongated but realistic and large roadrunner". The rule behind it, his: the machine "realizes that its records are interpretations.  it just doesnt understand that they might be fictional", and it holds the coyote "just as real" as anyone.

What realistic means to the narrator, as I put it and he confirmed at 19:00 ("your judgement on what realistic looks like to the narrator is correct"): real fur, flesh and blood, with enough of the old silhouette that the narrator can still know him on sight. How much of the cartoon's physics the body keeps is not spelled out.

## My read of the job

The chapters already hold strange things: a giant robot, a reptile, bat-faced goblins. A reader takes those as genre. The coyote is a quotation. He puts a new question in the reader's head: not where is this, but what made this.

It also breaks the narrator's own theory. In chapter 1 he guesses "some kind of alternate universe that I stumbled into". After this he needs a new one.

## The turn

Stephen's ladder for the narrator, 19:40, "the glacial reveal for the ontology of the narrator": "obviously, im real, like why would you even bring this up / shit, is that real / shit, thats a fucking cartoon monster / shit, thats an actual human being "just like me"". This scene carries the third and fourth rungs. The laugh is the third. The bodies are the fourth. The rung after the fourth is not in this scene: if they are just like him, he is just like them.

The scene is two beats with a gap between them: the laugh, then the bodies. In chapter 1's cat fight the jokes and the deaths came at the same moment, and the deaths got crowded. Here they come one after the other, on purpose. The gap is the scene. How long he laughs, and what exactly stops him, are the two things to get right.

## What I am listening for

His rule for the voice: "have it lapse into the absurd observations, the ludicrous comparisons, the more the narrator is panicking". Here the comparison is the thing in front of him. I want to see what his voice does after the laugh dies, when he has no joke left to reach for.

He is the only one present who knows it is meant to be funny.
