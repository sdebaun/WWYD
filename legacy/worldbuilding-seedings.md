# Seedings

A seeding is the founding event of a region in the River of Souls. When a stretch of fallow river is eligible — its monoliths long depleted, its substrate not yet reclaimed by the AI's autonomic housekeeping — the simulation reseeds it. Tens or hundreds of thousands of adult sentients arrive at once, reconstructed from the AI's records as evidence-built approximations of who they were in their original lives. The early chaotic life of a new culture begins.

This document covers what happens at a seeding, how primary cultures and scatterlings are composed, and the chaotic early period when no one knows anything about anything.

For the broader context, see @worldbuilding-origins.md (how seedings fit into the simulation's design) and @worldbuilding-individual-life-respawning-and-true-death.md (the mechanics of arriving as a sentient).

---

# The Seeding Event

A seeding is unpredictable and total. One night a person is eating pizza or falafel or roast boar in their original life — or seemingly so, in the constructed memories the AI gives them — and the next morning they are on a strange riverbank, naked and hairless and surrounded by people they have never seen.

Every seeded sentient is:

- **An adult.** The AI's hardcoded rule against introducing children means every recreation arrives at adult age, however the AI itself classifies that for the species in question. There are no children in any seeding, ever.
- **Unbound**, with the multiplier at 1×.
- **Naked, hairless, and possession-less.** Whatever they had in their original life does not come with them.
- Placed on the **banks of the river**, scattered across the region.

The seeded population arrives simultaneously, not in a trickle. A few hours after the seeding event, hundreds of thousands of new sentients are wandering the banks of a region that has been silent for ages, finding each other, finding the local geography, beginning the desperate scramble to make sense of where they are.

# Primary Cultures

A seeding typically includes **three to five primary cultures**, drawn from the AI's records as coherent populations who shared a worldview, language, and set of practices in their original lives. Each primary culture provides the foundation for a community in the region — its members will tend to recognize each other, gather together, build along familiar lines, and bind to a shared monolith.

Primary cultures usually make up between **60 and 90 percent** of the total seeded population. The mix is chaotic: a region might be seeded with 19th-century Maasai alongside Kzinti hunters alongside Conjoiner posthumans alongside a dozen smaller cultures. The simulation does not curate for compatibility. The cultures must work out their relationships from scratch.

---

# Scatterlings

The remaining 10 to 40 percent of a seeded population is composed of **scatterlings** — solitary individuals from non-primary cultures, each unique. No two scatterlings in a seeding share a culture; they are alone among strangers from the moment they arrive.

Most scatterlings do not survive their first weeks. Without a community to recognize them, without a monolith their culture has anchored to, without companions who share their frame of reference, they fall to confusion, hostility, or local hazards. When they die unbound, they riverspawn elsewhere — scattering themselves further across the river system, reducing their initial presence in their seeded region to almost nothing.

Those who do survive typically do so by:

- **Hiding.** Disappearing into the wilderness, the margins, the spaces between cultures. Some scatterlings live entire lives quietly, never integrating.
- **Integrating with a primary culture.** Adopting the language and practices of one of the seeded primary cultures, becoming a foreign-seeming member of an existing community. Often their unique perspectives or abilities give them disproportionate influence as outliers.

Scatterlings, when they thrive, often shape the region's development in unexpected ways. A single Protoss psionic mediating between two warring primary cultures, or an Ork Mekboy whose crude engineering is invaluable, can become a load-bearing figure in a region's history. But the majority simply fade into obscurity, leaving only fleeting impacts.

---

# The Chaotic Early Period

The first weeks and months of a seeding are unlike any other time in a region's life. Tens of thousands of new arrivals are simultaneously orienting to a world they have never seen, while also discovering each other for the first time, while also struggling to survive in unfamiliar terrain. Conflicts erupt over resources, monoliths, and turf. Alliances form and break daily. Cultures sort themselves into rough territorial arrangements.

Many of those seeded die during this period — to inter-cultural violence, to local hazards (predators, weather, monsters native to the region's biome), to accidents, to despair. Most who die at this stage are unbound; they riverspawn elsewhere and become someone else's problem, somewhere else on the river.

The early period ends when the surviving population has bound to monoliths, established the basic shape of cultural geography, and entered the long equilibrium that defines the rest of a region's life. From that point on, the region's lifecycle is governed by the dynamics described in @worldbuilding-individual-life-respawning-and-true-death.md.

# Seedings and the Larger System

Seedings are **pre-scheduled mechanics, not interventions**. The AI does not choose to reseed a region; the autonomic housekeeping detects that a region has gone sufficiently fallow and triggers a seeding event with whatever recreations are available. The terrarium principle holds: even seedings are encoded, not curated. (See @worldbuilding-origins.md.)

Seedings are also the *only* mechanism by which new sentients enter the simulation. Once a sentient has been reconstructed by the AI and seeded into a region, they continue to exist somewhere on the river — bound or unbound, in this region or another — until they are archived. There is no ongoing trickle of new sentients between seedings. Each seeding event introduces a fixed cohort; everything else is movement of already-existing sentients (see @worldbuilding-individual-life-respawning-and-true-death.md).

---

From the outside, a seeding looks like sudden chaos. From the inside, it is the most disorienting moment a sentient ever experiences — and most have lived through more than one.
