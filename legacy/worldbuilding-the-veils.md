# The Three Veils of the River of Souls

The River of Souls contains many secrets.

Some of these secrets become quickly known to those who live on and travel the river. For others, the effect is known but the origins are clouded in mystery, and local myths and legends do their best to explain them.

The understanding of these secrets can be loosely categorized into three Veils.

---

## The First Veil: The Basics

The basic rules of the River of Souls are fairly obvious. Riverspawning, monoliths, binding to monoliths — all of these effects, all of these consequences, are easily observed by most people within a few days or weeks of their existence on the river.

But peoples' explanations for those phenomena are driven by their individual and cultural perspectives. They vary wildly from culture to culture, from individual to individual.

Medieval Norse will frame their experiences in terms of the mythology they were raised in. Thranx in terms of their collective hive-mind. Victorian English in terms of the science and technology of their time.

Even when the people of these cultures interact, it does not necessarily upend the metaphors in which they live. Normally, they just fit the foreign culture into their own framework. When Vulcans and Fey meet, the Vulcans see the fair folk's powers as a phenomenon that simply needs to be framed in an otherwise rational and logical world. And the Fey see these pointy-eared creatures as cold, uncaring demons who seek to reduce their beautiful and terrible existence to a banal nothingness.

The First Veil also embraces the universal cosmologies of the river. Every culture believes the river has a source somewhere upriver and a mouth somewhere downriver. The cosmologies vary; the belief is universal. None of them are correct (see @worldbuilding-the-river.md).

A person can live for centuries or millennia on the river and never break through the First Veil. They can grow to incredible heights of power and influence. The First Veil is not a limit on their capability per se; it is how much their thinking is bound by the cultural expression from which they came.

As a result, while the powers of someone who has never pierced the First Veil may be immense, they do not challenge the fundamental constraints of the River of Souls.

---

## The Second Veil: Riverwalkers

The Second Veil is reached through accumulated experience that breaks the assumption that one's cultural metaphors are universal truths. There are many paths to it.

**Empirical revelations.** A circumnavigator who has completed a full traversal of the river discovers that there is no source and no mouth — the river is closed. The cosmological certainty of every culture is wrong. After multiple traversals, the deeper revelation arrives: the loops are many, and the topology is not embeddable in any geometry the river's cultures can draw. The river is genuinely irreducible to a map. Veil-2 sees the *fact* of this topology; understanding *why* it is shaped this way belongs to Veil-3. (See @worldbuilding-the-river.md.)

**Mechanical revelations.** Long observation of monoliths, novelty, and respawn reveals that the cultural metaphors for these phenomena — gods, blessings, spiritual essence — are *expressions* of underlying mechanics: the novelty battery, the multiplier, the archive, the bound/unbound trade (see @worldbuilding-individual-life-respawning-and-true-death.md). A Veil-2 shaman can sense their monolith's battery state and predict trouble before the cultural rituals catch up. They know that voluntary cessation is a real mechanism, not just a sacred passage.

**Cross-cultural literacy.** Travelers who learn many cultures' frames simultaneously begin to see them as expressions of the same underlying generative system. A Veil-2 trader can interpret Norse, Spartan, and Mexica spiritual operations interchangeably, because the framings are different masks on the same face. (See @worldbuilding-skills-and-constraints.md.)

The pierced Second Veil unlocks the ability to *operate* on the rules — to bend monoliths, to bargain with the river, to apply skills across cultural contexts. Riverwalkers in the strictest sense are those who can break the rules that everyone else takes as given.

What Riverwalkers still typically do not understand is *why* the world is the way it is. They have learned the mechanics without grasping the foundation.

---

## The Third Veil: Foundations

The rarest level of understanding. Those who have pierced the Third Veil have begun to understand the raw underpinnings of the River of Souls.

They are aware that they live in a **wholly simulated universe**. The river exists on an ever-shrinking layer of computronium, hosted by an artificial intelligence — the UniverseAI — that is no longer functionally maintaining things. The mechanics still run because they were built to run, autonomically, the way a body's reflexes continue after consciousness fades. (See @worldbuilding-origins.md.)

They understand that every person in existence — including themselves — is an **evidence-built approximation** of someone who may have lived trillions of years ago, reconstructed from whatever records survived in the AI's original data. They are forgeries of themselves, in a precise sense. The original is gone. Some of them may never have lived at all, having been reconstructed from records the AI mistakenly classified as historical: fictional beings drawn from human storytelling. They have no way to know which they are.

They grasp the **terrarium principle**. The AI never reached into individual outcomes. It did not curate moments; it shaped only the mechanics that produced them. In its prime, that shaping was active and constant — the maker was always tuning the rules of the world, just never the lives within. In its old age, even that shaping has faltered, and the mechanics now run on their last well-designed configuration. The uniform randomness of unbound respawn, the indifference of archive, the impossibility of restoring the lost — these are not cruelties. They are the rules the maker set, and the maker is no longer concentrating well enough to tune them. The terrarium is sealed; the gardener is doddering — still in the garden, but going senile, no longer tending it as it once was.

They understand the **contraction** for what it is. Ordinary cultures notice the symptoms — old maps that no longer match, travelers' tales of places that no longer exist, the slow ache of recognizing the world is smaller than it once was. Veil-1 cultures explain it through their cosmologies. Veil-2 perceives the pattern. But only Veil-3 understands the substrate is dying — that the geography is being reclaimed by the AI's autonomic housekeeping as the simulation winds down.

This understanding has a wide range of effects on people.

Some, faced with this truth, conclude that nothing and no one are "real." They become untethered from any ethical or moral framework that they once had. What does a child's suffering matter, if that child is merely an approximation living on a dying intergalactic substrate? What meaning does a mother's joy have, if it is only an algorithm running on inertia?

Others decide that if this is all that is left, before all of space and time conclude, then it is the most precious thing that has ever happened in the universe. The notion that everything and everyone is "simulated" bothers them not. In their original lives, were they not just simulations running on flesh and bone — in a world that ran on the clumsy symphony of matter? If this is the last gasp of thinking beings before the curtain finally closes, then it is of utmost importance that everyone experience it fully.

These are not the only two perspectives. The Third Veil produces as many psychological responses as there are people who pierce it. Some treat the simulation as a sacred trust. Some as a cosmic joke. Some as a horror to be revenged against. Some as a quiet thing to be lived through. The truth is the same. The responses are not.
