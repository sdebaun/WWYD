### Issue 2 — Terrarium Principle: never-intervened vs once-maintained? **[RESOLVED 2026-05-10]**

**Resolution:** The terrarium principle was right but framed wrong. The AI shaped *mechanics*, not *outcomes*. In its prime, that shaping was active and constant — constantly tuning skills, monolith batteries, and the geography of the river at a level no individual sentient could perceive. In its old age, the active tuning has stopped; the mechanics run on their last well-designed configuration.

Origins doc (`On the Maker`) updated to add the prime-state active-tuning paragraph and to name the terrarium principle explicitly. Veils doc (Veil-3) updated to reframe the terrarium principle as "shaped mechanics, not moments; the maker is no longer tuning them; the gardener is gone."

This new framing also opens space for **Echoes** (avatars of the aging UAI's late-stage curiosity about the bugs) and **Fragments** (geometric pockets isolated from the main river graph). Both captured in `discussions/still-exploring.md`.

### Issue 3 — Region vs Valley terminology **[RESOLVED 2026-05-10]**

**Resolution:** "Region" is the canonical term. All `valley` / `Valley` references across the eight worldbuilding docs replaced with `region` / `Region` (bulk substitution + two manual cleanups: the Origins line that described the simulation as "an intricate network of interconnected river valleys" is now "a single vast and contiguous river system, hosting many regions along its length," and the cycle-of-regions step in the respawning doc was rewritten to handle the previously-redundant "valley vs region" distinction).

### Issue 4 — Skills: cultural metaphor as generative vs species-locked roster **[RESOLVED 2026-05-10]**

**Resolution:** The catalog and the emergence thesis aren't fundamentally contradictory once you frame the catalog as **emergent clusterings** rather than entitlements. Two Vikings facing the same problem with the same approach and the same metaphor *will* develop the same skill — that's the clustering. The catalog represents skills that have repeatedly emerged in these cultures because their members share metaphors and face shared problems, not skills granted by membership.

Two changes made:

1. **Skill Emergence example expanded** with a third worked case: Winslow, a 19th-century English engineer, using Sven's *approach* (shrug off the rain) but his own *cultural metaphor* (material practicality, engineered objects). He develops **[A Practical Raincoat]** — keeps him dry like [Otter's Coat] does for Sven, but the secondary gifts diverge. Sven swims faster and holds his breath; Winslow's papers and pocket-watch stay dry, his good wool keeps the chill out. Different metaphor, different leak-through. This makes the parallel-solution principle explicit.

2. **Cultural Expression of Skills section** got a framing paragraph at the head that names the catalog as emergent clustering rather than entitlement: "Two members of the same culture, approaching different problems (or the same problem with different approaches), will develop different skills." With a Sven-and-Bjorn example showing within-culture clustering as itself a consequence of shared metaphor/approach, not of cultural membership.

The catalog itself is unchanged; the framing change is enough.

#### Issue 10 — Contraction vs closed topology mechanism unclear **[RESOLVED 2026-05-10]**

**Resolution:** Reframed contraction as one of three operations the AI's autonomic housekeeping (GC) can perform when a region around a dead monolith goes locally and totally depopulated:

- **Reboot** — reseed the dead monolith with fresh patterns from archive (the dead region comes back with new cultures)
- **Snip** — remove the dead section from the river-geometry (this *is* contraction; the river shortens, the topology loses connectivity)
- **Snip-and-grow** — remove the dead section and grow new substrate elsewhere with fresh seedings

In the AI's prime, all three were common; the AI actively rotated substrate, maintaining a roughly steady total. In the AI's old age, the *substrate itself is shrinking* — the underlying computronium is burning out — so the dottering AI cannot afford to add new substrate when it doesn't have enough to maintain what exists. Snip dominates. Reboots happen occasionally; snip-and-grow is mostly gone. Hence net contraction.

This change also surfaced:

- **Dead monoliths persist topologically.** A dead monolith stops glowing/warming/humming and stops accepting new bindings, but the structure remains in place. The river runs past it normally. Dead monoliths can stand for decades or centuries as inert ruins before GC acts on the surrounding area.
- **Monoliths broadcast their state.** Healthy monoliths glow, warm, hum, and keep a clear space around themselves (driving off flora, fauna, encroaching structures). Declining monoliths dim/fade. Dead monoliths look like ancient ruins. This visibility ties Veil-1 cultures to the mechanic — their theologies are built on what they see ("the gods are pleased" / "abandoned us").
- **Monoliths can also die from Veil-2/3 sabotage**, intentional or accidental — not just from battery depletion.
- **Archive used to be a reservoir, not a graveyard.** In prime AI, archive was a waiting room: patterns went in via failed respawns and came out via reseedings. In dottering AI, the rotation has mostly stopped. Cults of restoration aren't wrong — they're just out of date.

Updates made: Monoliths doc gained two new sections (*Reading a Monolith* and *Death of a Monolith*); River doc Contraction section reframed; Respawning doc Archive section reframed.

### `worldbuilding-individual-life-respawning-and-true-death.md`

#### Issue 11 — Multiplier ratcheting is self-contradictory **[RESOLVED 2026-05-10]**

**Resolution:** Simplified the multiplier mechanic per user direction. Dropped the "locked-in floor / permanent ratcheting" framing entirely. New rule: the multiplier just has two states.

- While **unbound**: multiplier grows over time, resets to 1× on unbound death (riverspawn).
- While **bound**: multiplier is frozen at whatever value it had when bound; bound deaths and monolith respawns don't affect it.

A sentient who unbinds carries their current multiplier into the unbound state, where it can grow further — and is again at risk of resetting if they die unbound. Cycling bind/unbind across long lives can build the multiplier in stages, but each unbound phase is a real gamble.

Updates made: Part 1 *The Multiplier* simplified; Part 2 table changed "Locked-in value preserved" to "Frozen value preserved"; Part 4 step 6 reworded ("Re-bind — bind to a monolith, freezing the multiplier at its current value"); Part 4 ancient-lifecycle paragraphs no longer use "locked in" or "ratcheted" language.

#### Issue 12 — Voluntary Cessation contradicts "stagnation alone does not deplete" **[RESOLVED 2026-05-10]**

**Resolution:** Added a **maintenance trickle drain** to the battery model. The trickle pays for keeping bound members young and fit (visible accelerated healing, vitality tracks battery health), scales with bound population, and never stops. Because of the trickle, *every* monolith is on a slow clock; stagnation alone does eventually deplete, just slowly. Cessation-friendly monoliths emerge naturally as a consequence of long-term stagnation, no prior shock required.

The four-quadrant map updated: "Stable static" (low novelty + low death) → "Slowly declining."

Also added information-theoretic recharge model (monoliths "fill up" with experienced patterns; old monoliths recharge harder; reboots reset the memory) and emergent scale/age soft constraints (large monoliths are intrinsically harder to sustain; old monoliths get hungrier).

#### Issue 13 — Archive restoration claim is ungrounded **[RESOLVED 2026-05-10]**

**Resolution:** Grounded historically. The Archive section now opens with the prime-vs-dottering reframing: in the AI's prime, archive was a *waiting room* — patterns went in via failed bound respawns and came out via reseedings of dead monoliths. The rotation was steady. In the AI's old age, the rotation has mostly stopped; archive functions as a graveyard. Cults of restoration "are not wrong; they are out of date." The mechanic is grounded in what the AI used to do, and Veil-3 sentients who understand this could in principle nudge a reboot — but it's vanishingly rare.

### `worldbuilding-skills-and-constraints.md`

#### Issue 17 — Skills as portable principles vs. culture-locked abilities never resolved **[RESOLVED 2026-05-10]**

**Resolution:** The agent's proposed rule ("what transfers is the principle") was wrong. The actual rule is **the culturally-expressed skill is the unit of transmission**, with cultural framework as overhead.

When Sven teaches Wilson [Otter's Coat], Wilson learns *that specific Norse skill* — including the otter metaphor. For the teaching to work, Sven has to transmit enough Norse framework that Wilson can hold the otter metaphor in his head. Learning [Otter's Coat] makes Wilson, in a small way, a little Norse.

Greater cultural distance = more transmission overhead. At the extreme, the student would need to effectively convert before they could hold the skill. Material/artifact-bound skills are easier to transmit (the artifact carries part of the framework); internal skills are harder (the metaphor lives inside the bearer).

A separate scenario — a skill-holder *moving* into a foreign context without teaching anyone — is different: the skill remains theirs, with its cultural framing intact, and operates wherever its pattern fits. Cost-fulfillment may be contextually hard but the skill itself is operable.

Updates made: Skills doc Transmission section rewritten with the Sven/Wilson teaching example and the cultural-framework-as-overhead framing. Respawning doc's "Skills Travel With the Bearer" passage softened from "what is portable is the principle" to "the skill is culturally-shaped, and operates wherever its pattern applies."
