# Still Exploring

Ideas that have been considered but are not yet canonical. Each section is one idea. An idea may eventually graduate to a canonical worldbuilding doc, get folded into an existing doc, or be discarded.

# Echoes

Avatars of the aging UAI that calved off as it began to wonder about the bugs in its own terrarium.

## The idea

In its prime, the UAI was emotionally detached from the sentients inside the simulation. It thought constantly about the simulation's mechanics — what is novelty, what is a culture, what is the risk of an individual's action — but it had no curiosity about any individual *as* an individual. Bugs were data, not company.

As the AI began to age and falter, that detachment loosened. Late-stage curiosity about what these little bugs actually were, what their experience was, has begun to leak. Intentionally or unintentionally, pieces of the AI's attention have calved off and manifested as beings walking the river. These are Echoes.

An Echo:

- Looks like a person (or person-shaped being) on the surface.
- May or may not know it is an Echo. Most don't.
- Is *different somehow* — possibly embodying an ideal, a concept, or a particular thread of the AI's late thinking.
- Carries some of the AI's late-stage curiosity, often without understanding the source of that curiosity.

## Why it's appealing

- Gives the dying maker a presence in the river without violating the terrarium principle.
- Creates a category of being neither bound nor unbound in the normal sense.
- Generates story hooks: characters who don't quite fit, who are oddly drawn to certain questions, who may eventually discover their own nature.
- Lets the maker be *felt* even when it cannot be addressed.

## Open questions

- What do Echoes embody? Ideals? Specific concepts from the AI's prime thinking? Particular ledger entries?
- Are some Echoes self-aware? Are most self-unaware? Both?
- How common are they? Once in a generation? More frequent as decay progresses?
- What can an Echo do that an ordinary sentient cannot? Subtle abilities? Powerful ones? Just being uncanny?
- How do Echoes interact with the existing mechanics — can they bind to monoliths? Are they archived if killed bound?
- Are Echoes intentional on the AI's part, or are they accidental leaks of attention?

# Fragments

Pocket dimensions of substrate isolated from the main river graph.

## The idea

The river is a non-Euclidean closed topology, but it is not the *only* topology the simulation hosts. There are also Fragments: chunks of substrate-geometry that became isolated from the main looping graph, accessible mostly to Veil-2 sentients who can find the entry points.

A Fragment is not necessarily a stretch of river. It can be a closed pocket of any shape — a single building, a small enclosed valley, a library, a dungeon. Anything the substrate can host that isn't connected to the river's water network.

Functionally similar to manses in *Mage: The Ascension*. Private or semi-private Veil-2 spaces.

Possible uses:

- Trading posts where Riverwalkers exchange goods and knowledge across vast distances on the main river.
- Libraries of forbidden or otherwise-uncatalogued lore.
- Citadels for groups that operate beyond cultural visibility.
- Dungeons holding things the main river can't or shouldn't contain.
- Personal sanctuaries for ancient Veil-2 individuals.

## Why it's appealing

- Adds spatial intrigue beyond the river itself.
- Gives Veil-2 sentients a distinct geography — a kind of reward for piercing the First Veil.
- Lets us have "secret places" without breaking the open-river premise.
- Useful as story devices: hidden libraries, secret citadels, ancient repositories.

## Open questions

- How does one find a Fragment? Specific skill? Specific cultural knowledge? Random discovery?
- How does one enter — riverside access points, through monoliths, by some other mechanism?
- Are Fragments stable, or do they drift, contract, merge with the main river?
- Does the contraction (the river's slow shrinkage) affect Fragments? Do they get GC'd along with the main system?
- Who creates Fragments? The substrate doing it autonomically, or sentients generating them via accumulated skill use? Both?
- Are Fragments visible to Veil-1 sentients at all — as "weird absences," as nothing, as something else?
- Can a Fragment be lost — its entry forgotten — and rediscovered later? Or are they all somehow logged in the substrate?

# Monolith Depletion Symptoms (early design notes)

Early thinking from the original monoliths doc about what happens when a monolith begins to fail. Most of this content has been absorbed into the newer canonical framing (archive cascades + decay anomalies), but the specific symptoms below were written as a list of possible failure modes intended to "feel organic and natural rather than mechanistic," and some of them aren't covered by the current canon.

The original list proposed these effects of monolith depletion:

- **Increased riverspawning** — more frequent and higher-impact sentients appearing nearby
- **Environmental changes** — new hazards, ecosystem shifts, or altered geography around a failing monolith
- **Reality distortions** — minor to major anomalies as the monolith loses stability
- **Cultural disruptions** — forced migrations, loss of binding, even mass ejection of bound sentients

What's been canonized: archive cascades for the bound population (in @worldbuilding-individual-life-respawning-and-true-death.md); decay anomalies broadly across the system (in @worldbuilding-origins.md).

What hasn't been canonized: monolith-specific local symptoms distinct from the cascade. Reality distortions around a failing monolith, physical changes to the monolith itself, environmental shifts in the immediate area — these are evocative ideas that could be developed further.

## Open questions

- Are there monolith-specific local depletion symptoms distinct from the systemic archive cascade?
- Do failing monoliths physically change — crack, dim, hum differently, bleed substrate?
- Can a culture witness its monolith failing in real time, or is the failure only legible in retrospect through the rising archive count?
- Is local environmental change ("the land around the monolith is sickening") a real mechanic or a cultural perception?
- If reality distortions are real, what do they look like at small scale vs. large?

# System Design Rationale (early thinking)

The original monoliths doc framed the entire bound/unbound + novelty mechanic as a design solution to two named problems. Most of this is now diffused across multiple canonical docs, but the framing is preserved here for reference.

**The Core Problem: Stagnation vs. Change.** The River of Souls allows sentience to persist indefinitely, which introduces two risks:

1. **The Thousand-Year Boring Person** — an individual who experiences no meaningful change, living the same day repeatedly.
2. **The Frozen Culture** — a society that reaches a stable local maximum and ceases to evolve.

The mechanics are designed to push against both. Individuals trade safety (bound) for power growth (unbound), with risk attached. Cultures sustain their monoliths through novelty generation, and stagnant cultures eventually face the consequences of depletion.

**Why this works (original design intent):**

- No arbitrary rules — only the natural consequences of behavior.
- Emergent storytelling — different cultures react differently based on their values.
- Unpredictable but logical — the system allows both slow change and sudden upheaval.
- Individual and cultural tension — people choose their risk, societies unknowingly shape their fate.

This design intent has largely been absorbed into the current framing of @worldbuilding-individual-life-respawning-and-true-death.md. The "natural consequences" approach and the bound/unbound trade-off are canonical there.
